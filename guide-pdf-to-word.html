How to Convert PDF to Word Without Losing Layout

Most PDF to Word converters get the words right and the layout wrong: headings jump to the wrong line, two-column text gets mixed into one column, and tables fall apart. That happens because a PDF does not store paragraphs the way Word does — it stores individual pieces of text at exact positions on a page. A good converter has to rebuild the layout from those positions instead of just dumping the text in reading order.

TrustFile does that with a layout-preserving mode. It is free, runs entirely in your browser, and your file is never uploaded — so you can convert a CV, a contract or an assignment without sending a private document to a server.

Convert your PDF in 3 steps

  1. Open the PDF to Word tool and upload your PDF (up to 50 MB). Drag and drop works, or click to browse.
  2. Keep “Keep the original page layout” checked. This is the default. Every line becomes an editable text box placed at its original position on a same-size Word page, with its font size and bold formatting kept.
  3. Download the Word file and check it before editing: skim the headings, the tables and any two-column sections. Small fixes are normal — the layout is preserved, but Word is still Word, and a long line may need a nudge.

Layout mode vs flowing text

Layout-preserving mode is the right choice when the page design matters — a CV, a letterhead, a form, or anything with columns and tables. If you plan to rewrite the document heavily and do not care where things sat on the page, uncheck the layout option instead: you get plain flowing text that reflows naturally as you type, which is easier to edit but loses the original arrangement.

What about scanned PDFs?

A scanned PDF has no text inside it at all — it is just a picture of a page. TrustFile detects that and runs text recognition (OCR) on the image, so you still get editable text. Two honest limits apply here: OCR can misread words on a blurry or low-quality scan, and a scanned page has no real layout information, so the result is flowing text rather than a pixel-perfect copy of the original. For the best result, scan at a good resolution and keep the page straight.

What does not carry over

TrustFile converts the text and its layout. Photos and coloured design strips from the original PDF do not carry into the Word file — only the text and where it sits. Slanted or curved decorations are also skipped. If a page is mostly images, use the PDF to Text tool for the words, or convert the page to an image with PDF to JPG instead.

Quick checklist before you send the file on

  • Check letter-spaced headings (like W O R K E X P E R I E N C E) — they should read as normal words, not joined into one.
  • Check that no line is clipped at the edge of the page, and that long lines did not wrap over the line below.
  • Check tables column by column — a table that looks right at a glance can still have a shifted cell.
  • If the PDF was a scan, read the OCR text once through: names, numbers and email addresses are the parts most worth verifying.
